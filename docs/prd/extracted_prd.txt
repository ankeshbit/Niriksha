AI-Assisted Legal MetrologyPackaged-Commodity Inspection System
Simplified Product Requirements Document (MVP)
Smart India Hackathon 2026 • Problem Statement 26034
Organization: Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution — Department of Consumer Affairs (DoCA)
1. Guiding Principle
This is an AI-assisted inspection and decision-support tool. It can read package labels, extract information and flag possible issues, but it does not make a final legal decision.
A human enforcement officer always makes the final inspection decision. If the AI or rule engine is unsure, the system must say “Needs Manual Verification” instead of guessing.
Important: OCR uncertainty is not a legal violation.
2. Executive Summary
The MVP helps Legal Metrology officers inspect packaged-product labels using images.
The system will:
Capture or upload package images.
Check whether the images are clear enough.
Use OCR to read text from the package.
Extract important declarations such as MRP, quantity and manufacturer details.
Apply a small, verified set of machine-checkable Legal Metrology rules.
Show the exact image evidence behind each finding.
Allow the inspector to correct, confirm or reject findings.
Generate a PDF inspection report after review.
Core workflow: Image → Quality Check → OCR → Extraction → Rule Check → Evidence → Inspector Review → Report
3. Problem Statement
Packaged products sold in India must show required information such as product name, manufacturer/packer/importer details, net quantity, MRP, date information and consumer-care details.
Manual checking is slow and can be difficult when many products and images have to be inspected.
The proposed system helps inspectors by automatically reading package labels, identifying possible issues and showing the evidence needed for human verification.
Authoritative source for actual legal requirements: Department of Consumer Affairs / Legal Metrology official sources.
4. USP and Key Differentiators
The USP is not simply “AI + OCR”. The main difference is the complete evidence-based inspection workflow.
Differentiator
Simple Explanation
Evidence-first findings
Every finding is connected to the original image, relevant image region, extracted value, confidence and rule.
Human verification
The inspector confirms, rejects or corrects findings. AI never makes the final legal decision.
Rule-version tracking
Each finding records the exact rule version used, so old inspections remain understandable after rules change.
OCR uncertainty handling
Unreadable or uncertain text goes to manual verification instead of automatically becoming a violation.
Audit-ready history
Images, AI results, rule results, inspector actions and report versions are preserved.
Legal Metrology focused
The workflow is designed specifically for Indian packaged-commodity inspections, not generic OCR.
Compared with a normal OCR scanner, this system connects extracted text to rules, evidence, human review and an inspection report.

6. Final Inspection Statuses
Status
Meaning
No Potential Violations Detected
All checks used by the MVP passed with enough evidence. This does not mean full legal compliance.
Potential Non-Compliance Identified
The inspector confirmed at least one potential issue.
Needs Manual Verification
One or more findings still need human review.
Insufficient Evidence
Images or coverage are too poor to evaluate reliably.
Not Applicable
Used when a specific rule does not apply to the product.
The word “Compliant” is not used as the final status because the MVP checks only a limited set of requirements.

13. Image Capture and Quality Checks
Camera capture and gallery/file upload.
Multiple images per inspection.
Tag images as front/back/side.
Preview, delete and retake images.
Check blur, brightness, glare, resolution, cropping, text size and missing package views.
Quality checks produce warnings only; they never create legal violations.
Example warning: “The back label appears blurry. Capture another image for reliable analysis.”
14. OCR and Extraction
OCR pipeline: Image → Preprocessing → Perspective correction → Contrast/noise processing → Text detection → OCR → Text + bounding boxes + confidence.
Preferred OCR: PaddleOCR. Tesseract may be used as an alternative.
Extraction uses Python regex/structured logic. An optional lightweight NLP/LLM fallback may help with difficult text grouping, but it is never required and never decides compliance.
Manufacturer/address parsing is best-effort. Low-confidence results go to manual verification.

16. Rule Engine
There are two separate types of checks.
16.1 Category A — Legal/Compliance Rules
These are machine-verifiable legal checks. Every rule must have an official source and provision reference.
If the provision is not verified, it is marked “Needs Legal Verification” and cannot produce a live legal finding.
16.2 Category B — Data Quality Checks
MRP is numeric and non-negative.
Quantity has a recognized unit.
Date has a recognizable format.
Phone number has a valid format.
Email has a valid format.
Data-quality failures are warnings, not legal violations.
16.3 Rule Result States
State
Use
PASS
Evidence supports the check.
POTENTIAL NON-COMPLIANCE
Enough evidence suggests a possible legal issue; inspector confirmation is still required.
WARNING
Data-quality or unusual-format issue.
UNREADABLE
Text cannot be read reliably.
NOT APPLICABLE
Rule does not apply.
NEEDS MANUAL VERIFICATION
Evidence is missing, unclear or conflicting.
When uncertain, the system defaults to “Needs Manual Verification”.
17. Legal Rule Verification Gate
No Category A legal rule can be enabled for live legal findings until its official source and provision are manually verified against the authoritative Department of Consumer Affairs / Legal Metrology source.
The system must never invent legal provisions.
AI/LLM-generated legal references are not authoritative.
Unverified rules stay marked “Needs Legal Verification.”
Only verified rules can produce live legal findings.
18. Evidence System
Every finding should connect the rule to the evidence that produced it:
Finding → Rule Version → Extracted Value → OCR Confidence → Source Image → Bounding Box/Crop → Explanation → Inspector Decision
The inspector should never receive a bare finding without supporting evidence when evidence is available.
19. Human Verification
Human verification is mandatory before a final report is generated.
Correct extracted text, MRP, quantity or date.
Enter a value manually when OCR says not_found.
Confirm or reject a finding.
Mark not applicable.
Add comments.
Request a new image.
The original AI/OCR result is immutable. Inspector corrections are stored separately with the inspector, time and action.

21. Report Rules
A final report cannot be generated while required findings are unresolved.
Unresolved examples: Needs Manual Verification, Unreadable, or Potential Non-Compliance waiting for confirmation.
If a finalized inspection needs correction later, reopen it and create a new report version. Older reports remain available.

28. Dataset Strategy
Legal knowledge: official Legal Metrology documents.
Product images: self-collected photos, properly licensed public datasets and staged test cases.

33. Edge Cases
Blur, darkness or glare → warn/retake.
Curved or rotated packaging → attempt correction; low confidence if unresolved.
Very small text → mark as difficult to read.
Unsupported language/script → extract what is supported; otherwise manual verification.
Partial label → request better coverage.
Multiple MRP values → flag conflict.
Sticker covering information → low confidence/manual verification.
Unreadable manufacturer/address → manual verification.
Duplicate/conflicting fields across images → show conflict.
Network failure → preserve draft where P1 storage is available.

44. Acceptance Criteria — Simplified
The following keeps the meaning of the original 35 acceptance criteria but uses simpler language.
Valid login creates an authenticated session/token.
Invalid login shows an error and does not create a session.
Valid inspection details create a Draft inspection with a unique ID.
Missing required inspection details show validation errors.
Supported images are stored with their view type.
Blurry images produce a quality warning before analysis.
Unsupported image files are rejected.
Analysis cannot start without an image.
Clear MRP text is extracted with confidence and bounding-box evidence.
Unreadable text is marked not_found, not guessed.
Low-confidence OCR is marked low_confidence.
Valid phone-like text can populate the phone field; invalid format becomes a data-quality warning.
Date-like text is extracted with a recognized-format indicator or marked unrecognized.
Ambiguous manufacturer text becomes low_confidence rather than a forced guess.
Different MRP values across images create a conflict requiring manual review.
Uncertain OCR sends the related rule to Needs Manual Verification.
A genuinely missing required declaration with sufficient evidence creates Potential Non-Compliance pending inspector confirmation.
A clearly present field with enough confidence can pass the applicable legal rule.
Rules that do not apply are marked Not Applicable.
Conflicting/ambiguous evidence requires manual verification.
Very low OCR confidence can produce UNREADABLE.
Data-quality failures appear as WARNING, not Potential Non-Compliance.
Unverified legal rules are visibly tagged Needs Legal Verification and cannot create live legal findings.
Opening a finding shows image evidence, bounding box, value, rule/version, explanation and confidence.
If an evidence crop cannot be created, show the full source image instead.
Confirming a finding stores the inspector decision and time.
Correcting a value stores the corrected value while preserving the original AI value.
Marking a finding Not Applicable removes it from the final issue count.
Requesting a new image causes the finding to be re-evaluated.
Unresolved findings block final report generation.
If all findings are reviewed and none are confirmed, the report says No Potential Violations Detected, never Compliant.
If an issue is confirmed, the report says Potential Non-Compliance Identified and includes the Legal Safety Statement.
Reports visually separate AI findings from inspector-verified findings and legal findings from data-quality warnings.
The P1 dashboard shows counts and summaries that match stored inspection data.
If the backend fails during upload, the image is not lost and a retry/local draft option is provided when P1 storage is implemented.
45. Final Safety and Readiness Checklist
AI never makes the final legal decision.
OCR uncertainty never automatically becomes a violation.
Every legal rule has a source/provision or Needs Legal Verification.
Only verified legal rules can produce live legal findings.
AI results, rule results, inspector decisions and final status remain separate.
Inspector verification is mandatory before a final report.
Original AI output is preserved.
The dashboard remains P1.
Offline work cannot delay the core MVP.
No unsupported legal provisions are invented.
The architecture remains a simple modular monolith.
The PRD is ready to freeze for development, subject to verification of the actual legal provisions used in Category A rules.
